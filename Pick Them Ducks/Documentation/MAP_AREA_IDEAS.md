# Duck park area ideas

## Small rocky mountain
A short switchback climb with broad terraces, shallow caves, and a summit nest. Put duck piles on shelves and in rock pockets. Players can collect up close or knock piles downhill toward a lower collection route. Keep one broad ramp usable by the roller car, with narrower jumping shortcuts for the player.

## Willow marsh and boardwalk islands
A shallow wetland with reed beds, drooping willow trees, little islands, logs, and branching boardwalks. Floating ducks reward careful movement and jumping; bushes conceal pockets of ducks. Place a casket near the main boardwalk junction, with space for the player to choose another installation site. Short loops should reconnect so a missed jump never means a long walk back.

## Overgrown orchard and picnic garden
Rows of shakeable fruit trees open into a picnic lawn with hedges, fallen baskets, and a small gazebo. Tree ducks, breakable bushes, and piles behind benches give several collection patterns in a compact area. Wide grass aisles suit the Sweeper and car, while little hedge openings reward exploration on foot.

## Abandoned mill and terraced stream
A small mill beside a stepped stream, with waterwheel scenery, wooden footbridges, and a dry yard full of duck piles. The yard is an easy bulk-collection area; the stream edges and mill steps hold smaller valuable groups. Place the shop/deposit route where the yard meets the main path so returning feels natural.

## Suggested arrangement
Keep the shop/depot in a central meadow. Put the orchard on one side for early exploration, marsh on the other for jumping and water collection, and the mountain behind them as the longest route. Connect all three with a broad outer path for the car, plus shorter footpaths through each area. Distinct silhouettes—rock summit, willow canopy, orchard rows—help navigation without a minimap.
